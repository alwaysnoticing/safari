Why early December?

Green-season landscape
Short rains (Nov–Dec) create lush plains, dramatic skies, and incredible light for photography. You're visiting during the most cinematic time of year.
Dramatically fewer crowds
Schools are still in session (holidays start late Dec). Tourist numbers at Ngorongoro Crater and camps are significantly lower. You'll have guides almost to yourself.
Calving season at Ndutu
Wildebeest and zebra are giving birth, creating calm, concentrated wildlife viewing. No chaotic river crossings — just mothers, calves, and predators in green grass. Serene and authentic.
Perfect for your travel style
You get exactly what you want: landscape + solitude + excellent guiding, without the Big Five circus.

Booking tips

1
Book directly or via trusted operators Asilia Africa, Nomad Tanzania, or Elewana. December discounts often available for shoulder season.
2
Request a landscape-focused guide When booking, ask camps to assign guides fluent in natural history and scenery, not just animal spotting.
3
Confirm Crater descent timing Request the 6:30–7:30 AM slot to avoid crowds and capture best light.
4
Ask about road conditions Post-rain roads can be muddy, which actually keeps tourists away — a feature, not a bug.
5
Internal flights are optional Arusha → Seronera airstrip is flyable but adds cost. Driving transfers work well and build anticipation.

Should I include Tarangire?

Short answer: No.
Tarangire is stunning but shines June–October when animals concentrate at the river. December = water spreads, wildlife disperses. Adding it would cut into your 5-night window and pull you away from your core route (Ndutu + Eastern Serengeti), which is designed exactly for what you want: landscape, solitude, and excellent guiding. Better to spend a second night at Osero and deepen that experience.

Bottom line: Your instincts are excellent. Early December in Tanzania is deeply underrated for travelers who want landscape and calm over animal density. The camps here prioritize atmosphere and guiding over thread count. This itinerary delivers exactly what you've asked for — and you'll have the Serengeti largely to yourself.

Who we are

We are two well-traveled individuals planning our first safari, but we are not approaching this as a once-in-a-lifetime checklist trip. As expats living in Europe, Tanzania feels accessible enough that we can imagine returning over time and experiencing different places, seasons, and landscapes in future visits.

For this first trip, we are less interested in chasing the Big Five, river crossings, or a highly polished travel-brochure version of safari. What draws us most is the beauty of Tanzania itself: the green-season landscape, dramatic skies, quiet open spaces, thoughtful camps, local culture, and the feeling of being present in a place rather than rushing through it.

Wildlife is absolutely part of the magic, and we would be thrilled to see elephants, giraffes, big cats, or anything else the landscape offers. But we do not want the trip to be built around expectations or sightings. We would rather have a calm, atmospheric experience with strong guiding, fewer vehicles, and time to appreciate the scenery, people, weather, light, and rhythm of the place.

Our ideal safari is comfortable, boutique, relaxed, and grounded — not ultra-luxury, overly formal, or performative. We are looking for a trip that feels authentic, beautiful, and personal: a first introduction to Tanzania that leaves room for discovery and makes us want to return.

The route
Seven nights,
three landscapes.

This is not a whistle-stop tour. Each location is chosen for atmosphere and access, with enough time to actually settle in.

1
Arusha
Arrival & first light
Fly into Kilimanjaro International Airport. Transfer to a quiet boutique inn on the Arusha outskirts — away from the town, with views of Mt. Meru. A gentle evening before the landscape begins.
2
Ngorongoro
The highland drive & crater rim
Three hours through highland forest. Arrive at the crater rim by mid-afternoon. Descend into the caldera at first light — 6:30 AM — before the convoys arrive. Spend the morning on the crater floor, then ascend for lunch and a quiet afternoon.
3
Ndutu / Southern Serengeti
Into the green plains
Transfer to Ndutu — a small, ten-tent camp on the edge of the conservation area. December is calving season: the plains are flush, the light is golden, and there are almost no other vehicles. One night here is never enough.
4–5
Eastern Serengeti
Two full days in the Serengeti
Move deeper into the Serengeti. Two nights at a seasonal tented camp near Seronera. Pre-dawn drives, long breakfasts, afternoon rest. December brings elephant herds to the woodlands and lion cubs to the kopjes. No rush, no checklist.
6
Seronera → Kilimanjaro
One last drive out
A pre-dawn game drive before the midday transfer to KIA — through acacia woodland and the long grass of the Seronera valley. Evening flight home, or onward.
